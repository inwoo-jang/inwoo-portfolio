Questions I think about

  • 2026.11.12

    What Should Change When AX Moves Across Industries?

    What changes across industries isn't the model. It's what breaks when things fail, which rules apply, and whether the result can be undone. That's why the same summarization, search, or execution task calls for different AI permissions and verification in each industry.

  • 2026.11.11

    As AI writes code faster, what needs to change in software development?

    Faster AI code generation does not speed up delivery to users when review, CI, merging, and deployment are backed up. Identify where delays occur, and track time to deployment, errors, and rework alongside code output. Drawing on company examples, this note covers development bottlenecks, responsibility for verification, and how to measure productivity.

  • 2026.11.11

    How can AI help clean up code?

    A lack of recorded calls is not enough reason to delete code. Even when AI identifies cleanup candidates, their original purpose, production usage history, and dependencies on other features still need to be checked. Drawing on a Codex case study, this note covers investigating unused code, completed experiments, and slow CI jobs, making changes in small PRs, and verifying their impact.

A pharmacy’s 1-, 2- and 3-year storage services came in as one order, and every box carried the same label with only the pharmacy name, order date and order number. Prescriptions inside had mixed issue dates. Operations staff typed orders in from Excel, and pharmacies requested partial or postponed pickups. As a result, statuses differed between the mall and our admin, scans were uploaded to the wrong order, and partial pickups were left out of settlement. I added the item line to the order number so each box got its own label, had the issue period written on the side of each box, and gave pharmacies a labeling guide. Now only picked-up boxes are settled, the mall shows partially shipped, and manual entry fell from about an hour to ten minutes a day.

Problem: pharmacies had to connect Hometax, the provider portal, social insurance and the card sales service one by one. Users: pharmacists and partner sales reps did the setup, with remote support when stuck. Constraints: external services kept their existing authentication methods. Our dev team and data provider adapted our connection flow, while CS and operations handled support. Approach: the three services that share a certificate became one flow, the card sales service kept its own account, and failed logins or personal certificates were given a separate path. Result: the flow went live and first-time setup rarely needed remote support.

OUTSIDE PUBLISH AREA

A fictional vitamin ad contains an efficacy headline, product copy, a 30% first-order discount and a 2+1 bundle with three separate conditions. OCR gives seven lines source IDs and hierarchy. The VLM reads the same image, OCR and user-edit snapshot, returning visual interpretations and three condition-to-claim links as candidates. Code accepts an exact quote only when it occurs once in its specified source line, rejecting absent or ambiguous quotes. Publishing-width changes recalculate display size without a model call. Cropped conditions are flagged, and small-print rereading returns a candidate without overwriting the original. This is a scripted demonstration of a local prototype, not a live model result or legal judgment.
